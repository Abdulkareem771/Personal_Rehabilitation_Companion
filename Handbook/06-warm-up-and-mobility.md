# Warm-Up & Daily Mobility

> "Warm-up is not preparation for exercise. It is preparation for healthy movement."

A proper warm-up prepares the muscles, joints, and nervous system for controlled movement.

For shoulder rehabilitation, the goal is **not** to become tired.

The goal is to improve movement quality before training begins.

The entire warm-up should take **5–10 minutes**.

---

# The Three-Step Warm-Up

Every session follows the same sequence:

1. Increase Blood Flow
2. Activate Stabilizing Muscles
3. Practice Movement Patterns

Never skip a step.

---

# Step 1 — Increase Blood Flow (2–3 Minutes)

Choose one:

- Easy walk
- Stationary bike
- Marching in place
- Slow treadmill walk

Intensity:

You should be able to hold a conversation.

Goal:

Warm muscles—not fatigue.

---

# Step 2 — Shoulder Mobility (3 Minutes)

Perform slowly.

Never force range of motion.

## Pendulum Swings

Duration

30 seconds each direction

Purpose

Lubricates the shoulder joint.

---

## Shoulder Rolls

10 forward

10 backward

Move slowly.

Focus on smooth circles.

---

## Scapular Circles

10 each direction

Move only the shoulder blades.

Avoid shrugging.

---

## Thoracic Extensions

8 repetitions

Use a foam roller if available.

Focus on extending the upper back.

---

## Wall Slides

2 × 10

Slow movement.

Maintain gentle pressure against the wall.

Stop before pain.

---

# Step 3 — Muscle Activation (4 Minutes)

These exercises "wake up" the stabilizing muscles.

The resistance should feel light.

---

## Band External Rotation

2 × 15

Light resistance.

Elbow stays beside the body.

---

## Band Pull-Aparts

2 × 12

Keep shoulders relaxed.

Move through the shoulder blades.

---

## Serratus Punch

2 × 12

Slowly reach upward.

Do not shrug.

---

## Scapular Retraction

2 × 12

Think:

Move the shoulder blades.

Not the arms.

---

# Readiness Check

Before starting the workout ask:

✓ Does my shoulder feel warm?

✓ Does movement feel smoother?

✓ Is pain below 2/10?

✓ Do I feel stable?

If the answer is "No," spend another few minutes warming up.

---

# Daily Mobility Routine (Morning)

This routine can be performed every morning.

Total time:

5 minutes

### Shoulder Rolls

10 forward

10 backward

---

### Neck Mobility

Slowly:

Look left

Look right

Look up

Look down

Five repetitions each.

---

### Thoracic Rotation

10 each side

Move through the upper back.

---

### Wall Slides

2 × 10

---

### Serratus Reach

2 × 10

---

### Deep Breathing

Five slow breaths.

Relax the chest.

Relax the shoulders.

Begin the day with good posture.

---

# Evening Recovery Routine

If the shoulder feels tired after work or training:

Perform:

✓ Pendulum Swings

✓ Shoulder Rolls

✓ Thoracic Mobility

✓ Gentle Walking

✓ Deep Breathing

Avoid stretching aggressively.

The goal is relaxation—not increasing flexibility.

---

# Warm-Up Rules

Never warm up with pain.

Never stretch aggressively before strength work.

Never fatigue the shoulder before training.

Always prioritize smooth movement.

If something feels unstable,

stop,

reduce resistance,

and reassess.

---

# The ReForge Warm-Up Checklist

Before every workout:

□ Body temperature increased

□ Shoulder mobility completed

□ Rotator cuff activated

□ Scapular muscles activated

□ Pain ≤ 2/10

□ Shoulder feels stable

□ Ready to begin

Never skip the warm-up.

A consistent 8-minute warm-up is one of the highest-return investments you can make in shoulder rehabilitation.
