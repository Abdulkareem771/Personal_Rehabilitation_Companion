# Pain Scale

The pain scale standardizes how symptoms guide exercise decisions.

Use this scale with the person's baseline, the exercise goal, and the next-day response.

## 0-2/10: Continue

Meaning:

- No pain, mild awareness, or tolerable discomfort.
- Movement quality remains controlled.
- Symptoms do not increase set to set.

Action:

- Continue the planned session.
- Maintain current load and range.
- Progress only if the next-day response is also stable.

## 3-4/10: Modify

Meaning:

- Symptoms are noticeable.
- The person may begin guarding or changing movement.
- The exercise may still be tolerable but needs adjustment.

Action:

- Reduce resistance.
- Reduce range.
- Slow the movement.
- Increase rest.
- Reduce sets or reps.
- Reassess after the next set.

## 5+/10: Stop Strengthening

Meaning:

- Symptoms are moderate to high.
- Movement quality or confidence is likely compromised.
- Continuing may increase irritability.

Action:

- Stop the strengthening exercise.
- Switch to low-irritability mobility or recovery work if appropriate.
- Reassess symptoms later that day and the next day.
- Do not progress the program.

## Red-Flag Symptoms

Stop the session and seek appropriate clinical guidance if symptoms include:

- New neurological symptoms.
- Chest pain, fainting, or severe dizziness.
- New traumatic injury.
- Severe or worsening night pain.
- Joint locking, major swelling, or suspected dislocation.

## Next-Day Rule

An exercise is only considered tolerated if symptoms return to baseline within 24 hours.
