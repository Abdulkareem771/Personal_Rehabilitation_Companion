# Progression Rules

Progression means increasing exercise demand only when symptoms, control, and recovery support it.

Progress one variable at a time.

## Progression Criteria

Progress when all are true:

- Pain stays at 0-2/10 during exercise.
- Symptoms return to baseline within 24 hours.
- Movement quality remains consistent across all sets.
- The final set looks similar to the first set.
- No apprehension, guarding, or unsafe sensation occurs.
- The person can complete the current prescription on at least two separate sessions.

## Progression Variables

Increase only one variable at a time:

- Reps.
- Sets.
- Hold time.
- Resistance.
- Range of motion.
- Speed.
- Complexity.
- Frequency.
- Sport or work exposure.

## Preferred Order

For most rehabilitation exercises:

1. Improve quality.
2. Increase range.
3. Increase reps or hold time.
4. Increase sets.
5. Increase resistance.
6. Increase speed or complexity.

## Do Not Progress When

Do not progress if:

- Symptoms are trending upward.
- Sleep or daily function worsens after sessions.
- Compensation increases.
- The person is relying on momentum.
- Apprehension is present.
