# Exercise Selection

Exercise selection matches the person's goal, phase, symptoms, equipment, and movement tolerance.

The best exercise is the one that trains the intended quality with the least unnecessary irritation.

## Selection Inputs

Use these inputs:

- Primary goal.
- Rehabilitation phase.
- Target joint or region.
- Movement pattern.
- Current symptoms.
- Irritability.
- Available equipment.
- Time available.
- Previous response to similar exercises.

## Phase-Based Selection

| Phase | Prefer | Avoid Until Ready |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stabilization | Mobility, isometrics, supported positions | High load, speed, provocative ranges |
| Strength | Predictable resistance, controlled tempo | Complex fatigue-based tasks |
| Functional strength | Integrated strength and trunk control | Sport-speed exposure without prerequisites |
| Return to sport | Specific exposure and higher capacity | Sudden jumps in load, speed, or contact |
| Maintenance | Efficient capacity-preserving work | Excessive daily rehab dependence |

## Symptom-Based Selection

- Low symptoms: choose the planned exercise.
- Mild symptoms: choose lower load or smaller range.
- Moderate symptoms: choose regression or mobility.
- High symptoms: stop strengthening and reassess.

## Equipment-Based Selection

Prefer exercises that can be performed consistently with available equipment.

Do not choose a gym-only exercise when a home program is required unless the program clearly offers alternatives.

## Selection Quality Check

An exercise is appropriate when:

- It matches the clinical goal.
- It can be performed safely.
- It can be progressed or regressed.
- It has clear dosage.
- It has an acceptable symptom response.
