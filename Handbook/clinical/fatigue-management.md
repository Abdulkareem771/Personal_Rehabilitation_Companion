# Fatigue Management

Fatigue changes movement quality, symptom response, and recovery.

Rehabilitation should build fatigue tolerance gradually without letting fatigue become the main driver of compensation.

## Useful Fatigue

Fatigue can be acceptable when:

- It feels muscular.
- Control remains stable.
- Pain stays 0-2/10.
- The person recovers normally.
- The final reps remain technically similar.

## Excessive Fatigue

Fatigue is excessive when:

- Form changes clearly.
- Breathing becomes strained during low-load work.
- The target muscle is no longer the main limiter.
- Compensation appears.
- Symptoms rise set to set.
- The next-day response is worse than baseline.

## Management Options

- Increase rest.
- Reduce reps.
- Reduce sets.
- Use lower resistance.
- Split the session into shorter blocks.
- Move technical exercises earlier in the session.
- Stop the session if quality cannot be restored.

## Programming Rules

- Motor-control work should happen before heavy fatigue.
- New exercises should be introduced when the person is fresh.
- Return-to-sport exposure should not be progressed under uncontrolled fatigue.
- Maintenance sessions should preserve capacity, not create repeated flares.
