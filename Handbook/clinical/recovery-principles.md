# Recovery Principles

Recovery determines whether training becomes adaptation or irritation.

Programs should consider the response during exercise, later that day, and the next morning.

## Recovery Markers

Useful signs:

- Symptoms return to baseline within 24 hours.
- Sleep is not disrupted by the trained area.
- Daily function is stable or improving.
- The person feels ready for the next planned session.
- Movement quality does not decline across the week.

## Poor Recovery Markers

Warning signs:

- Symptoms accumulate across sessions.
- Morning symptoms are worse than baseline.
- Sleep is disrupted.
- Normal daily activity feels harder after training.
- The same load feels unexpectedly more difficult.

## Session Spacing

General spacing:

- Low-load mobility may be daily if tolerated.
- Strength work usually needs recovery between sessions.
- High-load or sport exposure should be spaced based on response.
- New exercises should not be added in large clusters.

## Soreness Versus Irritation

Muscle soreness can be acceptable when it is mild, expected, and resolves.

Joint pain, instability, sharp pain, neurological symptoms, or worsening daily function should be treated as irritation, not normal training soreness.
