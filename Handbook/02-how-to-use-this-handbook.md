# How to Use This Handbook

This handbook is designed to be practical, not theoretical.

You are not expected to read it from beginning to end in one sitting.

Instead, use it as a reference that supports your rehabilitation journey.

---

# The Four Ways to Use ReForge

## 1. During a Workout

Open the exercise you are performing.

Focus only on:

- Exercise video
- Setup
- Execution
- Coaching cues
- Common mistakes

Complete your set.

Return to training.

The exercise page should never interrupt your workout.

---

## 2. Before a Workout

Review today's program.

Understand:

- Why each exercise is included
- What equipment you need
- How long the workout should take
- What today's objective is

This should take less than five minutes.

---

## 3. After a Workout

Record:

- Pain level
- Fatigue
- Stability
- Notes
- Questions

These records become valuable when reviewing your long-term progress.

---

## 4. During Recovery

Use the educational chapters to understand:

- Shoulder anatomy
- Rehabilitation principles
- Recovery timelines
- Progression criteria
- Common mistakes

Learning why an exercise matters improves consistency and confidence.

---

1. Summary

---

# The Rehabilitation Philosophy

Rehabilitation is not a race.

Progress is measured by:

✓ Better movement

✓ Better control

✓ Better confidence

—not by heavier weights.

The objective is to build a shoulder that remains stable during everyday life before demanding athletic performance.

---

# Listen to Your Shoulder

Some muscular fatigue is expected.

Sharp pain is not.

Mild muscle soreness is normal.

A feeling that the shoulder is slipping, shifting, or becoming unstable is not.

Quality of movement is always more important than quantity.

---

# Your Responsibility

ReForge provides guidance.

It cannot replace professional medical advice.

Use this handbook to support informed decisions, communicate more effectively with healthcare professionals, and remain consistent with your rehabilitation program.

The most successful rehabilitation programs are not the most intense.

They are the most consistent.
