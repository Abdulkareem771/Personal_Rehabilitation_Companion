# Shoulder Instability Program

This program organizes the existing exercise library into a phased shoulder-instability pathway. It is intended for controlled rehabilitation planning after irritability has settled and serious pathology has been clinically cleared.

## Phase Map

| Phase | File | Primary Goal | Typical Duration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [Stabilization](./phase-1-stabilization.md) | Symptom control, cuff activation, scapular positioning | 2-4 weeks |
| 2 | [Strength](./phase-2-strength.md) | Rotator cuff and scapular endurance | 3-5 weeks |
| 3 | [Functional Strength](./phase-3-functional-strength.md) | Integrated trunk, shoulder, and pulling strength | 3-6 weeks |
| 4 | [Return To Sport](./phase-4-return-to-sport.md) | Higher-load tolerance and sport-position confidence | 4-8 weeks |

## Program Principles

- Build control before load.
- Build endurance before speed.
- Keep shoulder work pain-free or low-irritability.
- Avoid repeated provocative end-range positions until the phase explicitly reintroduces them.
- Use completion criteria, not calendar time alone, to advance.

## Global Progression Rules

Progress only when:

- Pain remains 0-2/10 during exercise and returns to baseline within 24 hours.
- No repeated apprehension, slipping sensation, or protective guarding occurs.
- Scapular motion remains controlled through the assigned range.
- The final set looks similar to the first set.
- The person can explain which exercise or position is most provocative.

## Global Regression Rules

Regress if:

- Symptoms rise above 3/10 or linger the next day.
- The shoulder feels unstable, loose, or unsafe.
- Neck, upper-trapezius, or trunk compensation becomes the main strategy.
- Sleep, dressing, desk work, or daily reaching worsens after sessions.

## Clinical Reassessment Triggers

- New trauma or dislocation event.
- Increasing night pain.
- Neurological symptoms, numbness, or progressive weakness.
- Failure to improve after 3-4 weeks of consistent phase-appropriate work.
