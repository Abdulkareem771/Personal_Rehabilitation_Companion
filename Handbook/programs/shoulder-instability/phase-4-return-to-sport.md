# Phase 4: Return To Sport

## Objective

Prepare the shoulder for repeated loading, sport-position exposure, and higher confidence while preserving the control developed in earlier phases.

## Duration

- **Typical length:** 4-8 weeks.
- **Frequency:** 2-3 strength sessions plus 1-2 sport-specific exposure sessions per week.
- **Session length:** 40-60 minutes.
- **Progression gate:** Sport-specific work increases gradually without apprehension or delayed flare.

## Weekly Schedule

| Day | Focus | Exercise Order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Strength | Mobility, cuff primer, primary pulling, scapular endurance |
| Day 2 | Sport exposure | Low-volume technical practice outside this exercise library |
| Day 3 | Recovery | Thoracic mobility and posture reset |
| Day 4 | Strength | Cuff, pulldown or row, face pull, trunk |
| Day 5 | Rest | Symptom review |
| Day 6 | Sport exposure | Gradual position and intensity exposure |
| Day 7 | Rest | Weekly readiness review |

## Sport Exposure Rules

Sport-specific drills are documented outside the current exercise library and should be added later as dedicated exercise files before the app loads them as formal content.

Until those files exist:

- Record exposure type, volume, intensity, and symptom response in notes.
- Increase only one variable per week: duration, intensity, range, speed, or contact.
- Keep the first exposure of any new position low volume.
- Avoid fatigue-based testing if apprehension is present.

## Progression Rules

Progress when:

- Strength sessions remain stable with no next-day symptom increase.
- Sport-position exposure feels controlled and repeatable.
- The person can stop a repetition or drill without protective guarding.
- Apprehension is absent or clearly decreasing week to week.

## Regression Rules

Regress when:

- Return to Phase 3 if sport exposure creates repeated apprehension.
- Reduce sport volume before reducing strength work if gym sessions remain well tolerated.
- Remove pulldown temporarily if overhead or elevated arm positions are provocative.
- Use chest-supported pulling before free-standing pulling when fatigue affects control.

## Clinical Checkpoints

- **Pain:** 0-2/10 during strengthening and no delayed increase after sport exposure.
- **Control:** Shoulder position remains organized under fatigue.
- **Confidence:** No avoidance of expected sport positions at the current exposure level.
- **Recovery:** Baseline symptoms return within 24 hours after each exposure.
- **Capacity:** Weekly training load is consistent without accumulating irritation.

## Completion Criteria

The program is complete when the person can perform normal sport-specific practice at planned intensity, complete two weekly strength sessions, and maintain stable symptoms for at least two consecutive weeks.
