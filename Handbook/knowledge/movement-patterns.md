# Movement Patterns

Movement patterns describe reusable movement concepts. They should be referenced by exercises instead of re-explained on every page.

## Scapular Retraction

Scapular retraction is the movement of the shoulder blade toward the spine.

Use it to describe controlled pulling, posture resets, and mid-back activation. It should not be forced into excessive pinching or paired with rib flaring.

## Scapular Depression

Scapular depression is the movement of the shoulder blade downward on the rib cage.

It can help reduce excessive shrugging, but it should not become rigid bracing that blocks normal upward rotation.

## Scapular Upward Rotation

Scapular upward rotation occurs when the shoulder blade rotates so the socket supports arm elevation.

It is important for comfortable reaching and overhead motion. Healthy upward rotation usually works with posterior tilt and thoracic control.

## Scapular Protraction

Scapular protraction is the movement of the shoulder blade around the rib cage away from the spine.

It supports reaching, pushing, and serratus anterior activation. It should feel controlled rather than collapsed.

## External Rotation

External rotation turns a limb away from the midline or rotates the shoulder outward.

At the shoulder, it is often used to train posterior rotator cuff control and support joint positioning.

## Internal Rotation

Internal rotation turns a limb toward the midline or rotates the shoulder inward.

At the shoulder, it can train anterior cuff control and functional reaching patterns when dosed appropriately.

## Neutral Spine

Neutral spine means the spine is organized near its natural curves without excessive rounding, arching, or twisting.

It is a control target, not a rigid position. Breathing should remain possible.

## Hip Hinge

A hip hinge is a movement pattern where the hips move backward while the trunk stays organized.

It emphasizes hip motion and posterior-chain loading while limiting unnecessary spinal motion.

## Bracing

Bracing is the controlled creation of trunk stiffness to support movement.

Effective bracing allows breathing, avoids excessive strain, and matches the task demand.

## Closed Chain

Closed-chain movement occurs when the hand or foot is fixed against a surface.

It often increases joint compression, feedback, and whole-body coordination. Difficulty depends on load, angle, and body position.

## Open Chain

Open-chain movement occurs when the hand or foot moves freely through space.

It can isolate a region more easily and allows precise control of range and resistance.

## Plane Of Motion

- **Sagittal:** Forward and backward movement.
- **Frontal:** Side-to-side movement.
- **Transverse:** Rotation.
- **Multi-plane:** Combined motion across more than one plane.
