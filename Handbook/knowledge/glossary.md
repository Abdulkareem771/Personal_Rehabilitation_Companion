# Glossary

This glossary defines common rehabilitation terms used across the handbook.

## Apprehension

A feeling that a joint may slip, give way, or become unsafe during a movement. Apprehension is especially important in instability programs and should be treated differently from normal muscular effort.

## Baseline

The person's usual symptom level before a session or intervention. Progression and flare-up decisions should compare symptoms to baseline, not only to a single moment during exercise.

## Capacity

The amount of load, volume, speed, or range a person can tolerate while maintaining control and recovering normally.

## Compensation

A movement strategy that shifts work away from the intended area. Examples include shrugging during shoulder work, arching the low back during overhead motion, or twisting the trunk during rotation exercises.

## Contraindication

A reason an exercise should not be performed or should be delayed until clinical review.

## Irritability

How easily symptoms are provoked and how long they take to settle. High irritability means smaller loads, shorter sessions, and more conservative progression.

## Load

The total demand placed on the body. Load can include resistance, range of motion, speed, time under tension, number of sets, training frequency, and sport exposure.

## Motor Control

The ability to coordinate a movement with appropriate timing, position, and muscle activity. Motor control is not the same as maximal strength.

## Range Of Motion

The available movement at a joint or body region. Range may be active, passive, loaded, unloaded, comfortable, or provocative.

## Regression

A planned reduction in difficulty used to preserve quality, reduce symptoms, or continue training safely.

## Repetition

One complete performance of a movement or one prescribed hold.

## Set

A group of repetitions or a sustained time block completed before resting.

## Symptom Response

How symptoms behave during exercise, immediately after exercise, later that day, and the next day.

## Time Under Tension

The total time a muscle or joint is loaded during a set. Slower tempo and longer holds increase time under tension.
