# Posture Principles

Posture is a flexible relationship between position, load, time, breathing, and task demand.

The handbook treats posture as trainable behavior, not a single perfect shape.

## Key Principles

- No single posture must be held all day.
- Movement variability matters.
- Comfort often improves when positions change regularly.
- Strength and endurance support posture better than forceful correction alone.
- Breathing and rib position influence neck, shoulder, and trunk tension.

## Desk Work

Useful desk-work strategies include:

- Change position before discomfort becomes intense.
- Keep frequently used items within easy reach.
- Support the forearms when helpful.
- Use short mobility breaks rather than waiting for a long session.
- Avoid forcing the shoulders down and back all day.

## Training Context

During exercise, posture should support the goal of the movement.

Good training posture usually means:

- The target area is working.
- Breathing remains controlled.
- Joints feel stable.
- Compensation is limited.
- The person can repeat the position without excessive effort.
