# Rehabilitation Phases

Rehabilitation phases provide shared language for exercise difficulty, program structure, and clinical progression.

Phases describe intent and readiness. They are not rigid timelines.

## Phase 1: Stabilization

Purpose:

- Reduce irritability.
- Restore confidence in safe ranges.
- Establish low-load motor control.
- Build tolerance to frequent, short sessions.

Typical features:

- Mobility.
- Isometrics.
- Low-load activation.
- Small ranges.
- Conservative symptom rules.

## Phase 2: Strength

Purpose:

- Build basic strength and endurance.
- Introduce predictable resistance.
- Preserve movement quality under mild fatigue.

Typical features:

- Bands.
- Light dumbbells.
- Controlled tempo.
- Repeated sets.
- Clear rest periods.

## Phase 3: Functional Strength

Purpose:

- Integrate the target region with trunk and whole-body movement.
- Increase load tolerance.
- Prepare for daily, gym, or work demands.

Typical features:

- Compound movements.
- Supported gym work.
- More total volume.
- Greater fatigue tolerance.

## Phase 4: Return To Sport Or Performance

Purpose:

- Reintroduce higher speed, range, load, and task-specific exposure.
- Build confidence in demanding positions.
- Prepare for repeated sport or performance tasks.

Typical features:

- Gradual sport exposure.
- Higher-load strengthening.
- Position-specific preparation.
- Careful recovery monitoring.

## Maintenance

Purpose:

- Preserve capacity after formal rehabilitation.
- Reduce recurrence risk through consistent low-volume work.
- Identify early warning signs before a flare becomes persistent.
