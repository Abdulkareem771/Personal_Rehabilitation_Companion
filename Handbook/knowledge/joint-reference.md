# Joint Reference

This reference standardizes joint terminology used by exercises, programs, and future clinical logic.

## Shoulder Complex

The shoulder complex includes the glenohumeral joint, scapulothoracic articulation, acromioclavicular joint, sternoclavicular joint, thoracic spine, and rib cage contribution.

Clinical reasoning should consider the whole complex rather than treating the ball-and-socket joint in isolation.

## Glenohumeral Joint

The main ball-and-socket shoulder joint. It provides large range of motion and depends heavily on muscular control, capsule integrity, and coordinated scapular motion.

## Scapulothoracic Articulation

The movement relationship between the shoulder blade and rib cage. It is central to shoulder mechanics, posture, pushing, pulling, and overhead movement.

## Thoracic Spine

The upper and mid-back region. Thoracic extension and rotation can influence shoulder position, rib position, breathing, and desk-work tolerance.

## Cervical Spine

The neck region. Neck symptoms, nerve symptoms, or protective tension can affect shoulder and upper-back exercise tolerance.

## Lumbar Spine

The lower-back region. Lumbar position can influence bracing, hip hinging, overhead compensation, and loaded exercise control.

## Hip

The ball-and-socket joint connecting pelvis and femur. Hip strength and control support lower-body training, gait, trunk position, and whole-body loading.

## Knee

The joint between femur and tibia. Knee loading should consider hip and ankle contribution, not only local knee motion.

## Ankle And Foot

The ankle and foot support balance, gait, squatting, and lower-body loading. Foot position can affect the entire kinetic chain.

## Metadata Guidance

Use the `joint` field to identify the main joint or region the exercise is intended to influence.

Examples:

- `shoulder`
- `scapulothoracic`
- `thoracic-spine`
- `lumbar-spine`
- `hip`
- `knee`
- `ankle`
