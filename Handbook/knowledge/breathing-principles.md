# Breathing Principles

Breathing influences bracing, rib position, neck tension, and exercise tolerance.

Breathing guidance should match the exercise goal and the person's symptom response.

## General Rules

- Avoid unnecessary breath-holding during low-load rehabilitation.
- Exhale during effort when coordination is difficult.
- Keep the neck and jaw relaxed when possible.
- Use breathing to reduce guarding after painful or unstable movements.
- Bracing should not prevent normal breathing unless a specific high-load task requires brief pressure.

## Breathing And Bracing

Bracing creates trunk support. Breathing maintains pressure control and reduces excessive tension.

A useful brace feels:

- Firm enough to support the task.
- Light enough to breathe behind.
- Distributed around the trunk, not only in the neck or low back.

## When To Modify

Modify breathing strategy if:

- The person holds breath during every repetition.
- Neck tension increases.
- Dizziness occurs.
- Rib flaring replaces trunk control.
- Bracing creates shoulder guarding.
